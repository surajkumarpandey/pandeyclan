Bairati, Almora — the ancestral village

In the Kumaon Himalayas of Uttarakhand, once part of the Katyuri and later Chand kingdoms. Open in Google Maps · See it on the journeys map

The region

Almora was the seat of the Katyuri dynasty from around the 9th century, then capital of the Chand kings from 1560 onward — the political and cultural heart of Kumaon for centuries. Unlike Shimla or Nainital, Almora was never built up by the British; it's a hill station shaped entirely by Kumaoni hands, known for its temples, its stone-and-wood architecture, and its folk music.

Bairati itself is small enough that it doesn't appear in indexed histories — which is normal for a hamlet, not a gap in research. What's known of it lives in the family's own memory, and in the land records below.

Aipan — the art of this land

Aipan is the ritual floor-and-wall art of Kumaon: geometric patterns — lotuses, chowkis, temple motifs, borders of triangles and dots — drawn freehand in rice-paste white (biswar) on a base of red ochre (geru). Traditionally made at thresholds, in worship spaces, and for festivals and ceremonies, it's one of the most recognisable visual traditions of this region. This site's colour palette and border patterns are drawn from it.

Finding family land records

Uttarakhand's official land records portal covers Bairati under these exact divisions:

जनपद (District)अल्मोड़ा — Almora
तहसील (Tehsil)चौखुटिया — Chaukhutiya
ग्राम (Village)बैरती — Bairati
  1. Go to bhulekh.uk.gov.in (the official portal — I can't fetch it automatically for you, it blocks automated access and needs a CAPTCHA, so this is a manual visit).
  2. Select district अल्मोड़ा, tehsil चौखुटिया, village बैरती.
  3. Choose search by Khatedar ka naam (owner's name) — enter it in Hindi/Devanagari for best results. If you don't have a khasra number, the name search is the way in.
  4. Try the earliest ancestor first — Shri Sukhdev, or one of his sons. Land records often still list an earlier generation's name as the registered holder, even now.
  5. Once you find a khata, note the khasra numbers — bhunaksha.uk.gov.in will show the actual plot boundaries on a map for the same district/tehsil/village.

Whatever you find is worth adding to the relevant person's bio or locations field from the Edit page.
